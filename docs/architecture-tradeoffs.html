Architecture & Trade-offs

ledger-core: an in-memory, append-only, value-dated account ledger · Part 2 of the take-home
Jeffrin Mathew Benny · 27 September 2026 · github.com/jeffrinmb/mal-assessment
Every point below refers to the implementation (src/ledger.js, src/constants.js), not to a hypothetical system. One constant runs through all four sections: the back-value window N (BACK_VALUE_WINDOW_DAYS). It bounds how far history can be restated. That makes it the line for what can be cached, what can be pruned, and what needs a second approver.

1Append-only at scale

What breaks first at 100×: day close, on CPU, before memory

  • balance() is a linear scan of every entry.
  • #accrueInterest calls it for every account × every value day since the window began, and filters the whole accrual list for each comparison. One close costs about accounts × days elapsed × (entries + accruals), and the cost grows every day.
  • #afterPosting re-runs fee assessment for all accounts when one account receives a backdated entry.

At 100× volume the close goes from milliseconds to hours. The authorization path degrades too. authState() scans the transition log, and activeHolds() calls it once per auth, so every approval is O(auths × transitions) on the latency-critical path.

Where state accumulates without bound

The entry log is meant to grow; that is the contract. The problem is that the working state lives as long as the audit record:

StructureGrows withPruned?
#accrualsaccounts × days, plus adjustment recordsNever, not even after capitalization
#auths, #authLogevery auth, terminal ones includedNever
#feeKeysaccounts × negative daysNever
#dayReportsdays × accounts, each copying that day's entriesNever
#diagnosticsevery error and warningNever
Cheapest structural change

A frozen checkpoint at today − N. The value-date guard already refuses anything older than N days, so state before today − N can never change again.

  1. Checkpoint: keep one closing balance and one accrual total per account at today − N. Balance queries fold in only the entries inside the window.
  2. Scoped loops: fee and interest loops walk [today − N, today], and only for accounts touched since the last close. Restatement cost drops from O(history × all accounts) to O(N) per affected account.
  3. Auth projection: a derived authId → state map, updated as transitions are appended. Terminal auths older than N leave the hot map.
  4. Archive: fee keys, accruals and snapshots older than the checkpoint go to archive storage.

The log stays the source of truth, and checkpoints are rebuildable caches. The change adds no new guarantee; it only uses one the guard already enforces. The log's own growth is left as a storage-partitioning problem (by account and value month), not a memory problem.

2Value-dated entries in production

A back-valued entry changes facts that customers, finance and regulators have already relied on. In a CBUAE-licensed bank that creates the following surface:

AreaExposure
Statements & feesAlready-issued statements become wrong. Fees appear for past days the customer could not have seen or avoided. The CBUAE Consumer Protection Regulation and Standards require fees to be transparent and fair, so retroactive charging needs an explicit policy. Today this design charges such fees and never refunds them.
Interest & profitAccrual adjustments are correct inside the ledger. But interest already paid, reported on a statement or tax certificate, or distributed as profit on an Islamic product has to be corrected across a period boundary.
GL & regulatory reportingA value date in a closed month changes figures already reported: management accounts, IFRS 9 inputs, and prudential returns filed with CBUAE. Finance must decide between a prior-period adjustment and a current-period correction.
Financial crimeMonitoring usually runs on booking time, so back-valuing opens a gap between when money moved economically and when monitoring saw it. Unexplained back-valuation is also a classic insider-fraud pattern: an overdraft erased, interest shifted, a fee avoided. goAML reports must carry both dates.
Irreversible decisionsAuth approvals and declines were made on the balance known at the time, and restatement cannot undo them. Complaints cite the balance the customer saw, so both views must be reproducible. That is what balance(acc, day, {bookedThrough}) provides.
AuditEvery restated figure must trace to the entry that caused it, the person who booked it and the reason.
Control to add before go-live

Back-value authorization with an impact record.

  • Every entry dated before its booking day carries a mandatory reason code.
  • Beyond N days, or into a closed accounting period, the entry needs a second approver (maker-checker) before it posts.
  • On posting, the system writes an impact record linked to the entry: every fee, accrual adjustment and restated balance it triggered. Operations uses that record to decide on refunds and customer notices.

The hard limit already exists in code (BACK_VALUE_LIMIT). In production, a breach goes to a checker queue instead of being refused.

3Authorization lifecycle

In this model an authorization can end in three ways other than a matching settlement:

EndingIn codeReal-world scenarioBehavior I would mandate
Declined at birthauthorize() → DECLINEDInsufficient available balance, or a risk decline.Record the decision with the available balance it was judged on (done). Place no hold. Route any later settlement against it to suspense (done). Return a reason code to the scheme.
Expired#expireHolds() → EXPIREDThe merchant never clears: an abandoned order, or a fuel pre-auth never completed.Release the full hold after the scheme period, driven by a scheduler. Here expiry runs only inside closeDay(), so an inactive account keeps its hold forever (the known-failing test). A settlement after expiry goes to suspense, and ops force-posts it where the scheme obliges payment.
Remainder released on under-settlementsettle() records releasedThe final amount is below the authorized amount (fuel, hotels, restaurants).Release the difference in the same transition (done). For merchant categories that clear in several pieces, keep the remainder open until expiry.

Situations that leave an auth open

Over-settlement: the settlement goes to suspense and the auth stays ACTIVE. The mandate is a tolerance per merchant category (tips, FX movement). Within tolerance, settle and debit the excess. Above it, go to suspense and chargeback review, and close the hold so it cannot be released and then charged again when ops force-posts.

Active at window end: this is expected. The hold keeps reducing available balance until the expiry scheduler picks it up.

Endings the model lacks

Merchant reversal or void: a REVERSED state, idempotent because reversals are retried.

Incremental authorization: increase the existing auth, and check only the increment against available.

Account freeze or closure: keep holds on a frozen account and route its settlements to review. Block closure while holds are open.

The rule behind all of these: an auth ends only through an explicit, logged transition, and a hold is never quietly dropped. The transition log already has that shape. What it lacks is a clock.

4What I cut and why

CutWhyProduction risk deferred
Double-entry and GLThe brief is about customer balances. Only a one-sided suspense account exists.The largest gap. Nothing proves the books balance: suspense, fees and interest have no contra-legs, so nothing reconciles to the GL.
PersistenceOut of scope by instructionA crash loses everything. Freezing objects only proves immutability within a single process.
ConcurrencyA single ordered streamThe check-then-hold in authorize() is a race: two concurrent auths can both pass on the same funds. It needs a per-account serial queue or a conditional write.
Inbound idempotencyThe stream is replayed onceA redelivered event posts again, because ref is not a unique key.
Hold-expiry clockOnly event-driven closes existUnsettled holds block funds on inactive accounts indefinitely.
Calendar, time zones, cut-offs"Day" is an integerNo weekends, holidays or cut-off times. AED and BHD books have different local cut-offs, so "end of day" is undefined across them.
FXNot needed in-windowA negative non-AED account raises an error and is charged no fee: a revenue leak until an FX rate policy exists.
Fee refund policyNo rule was givenCustomers keep fees caused by entries the bank later reversed: a conduct and complaints risk.
Multi-clearing, increments, over-settlement toleranceSingle clearings onlyHotel, fuel and tip flows land in suspense and need manual work.
Forward-dated entriesNot neededScheduled payments cannot be modeled. They are refused today.
Debit interest, limits, account statesOut of scopeOverdrafts are priced only by the flat fee. There is no limit, freeze or closure.
Indexes and checkpointsCorrectness firstDay close is super-linear in history (§1).
Day-count conventionA flat daily rateProducts quoted as an annual rate on Actual/365 or Actual/360 would accrue differently.
Deliberately not cut

Integer minor units · a single rounding point · capitalization defined as the sum of the rounded accruals · frozen records with derived balances · the bitemporal balance query · suspense instead of silently dropping entries. Each cut above leaves the system incomplete. Getting any of these wrong would make it incorrect.
